# Requirement: RQ-005

## Title

Producing the weekly edition must be one decision, and approved work must never
disappear

## Description

Producing an edition today requires a person to understand the machine. They must
know that collection has to be started by hand, that the results land in a queue,
that approving an item removes it from the screen they are on and surfaces it in a
different section, and that an edition must then be assembled, previewed and sent
as separate acts. Nothing in the interface explains this order.

Two things make it worse than merely unexplained.

**The same list is shown twice.** The Feed screen and the Review queue read the
same data: both request the articles awaiting a decision. They differ in layout
and in nothing else. A person moving between them cannot tell whether they are
looking at two things or one, and doing the work in one silently empties the
other.

**Approving gives no sign of where the work went.** An approved article leaves
both screens at the moment it is approved, and nothing on either screen says where
it has gone. It is in fact counted on the Editions screen, under "approved and
waiting", and can be added to an edition from there. But that is a different
section, which a person has no reason to open straight after approving, and no
message points them at it. From the seat of the person doing the work, approving
removes the story and says nothing, which reads as losing it.

> **Correction.** This section first said the article "appears on none" and existed
> only inside the builder's picker. That was wrong: `app/dashboard/send/page.tsx`
> loads approved articles and splits them into those waiting and those already
> used. The defect is feedback and navigation, not visibility, and the distinction
> matters: whoever implements this should not build a screen that already exists.

The intent is the opposite of this. A business user should open the product once a
week, see a proposed edition already assembled from what the machine collected and
scored, read it, and approve it. Approving should send it. Everything before that
moment is the machine's work, and should be visible as status rather than as a
sequence of buttons a person has to know the order of.

The intermediate states must remain reachable for the editor who wants to
intervene: what was collected, what was scored and rejected, what is approved and
waiting. But they become places you can go to look, not stations you must pass
through in the right order for the product to work at all.

## Source

Raised by Julian Andrade after using the product as a business user would: "first
we need to run curation then that run should add the results to the feed but when
you approve they disappear from the feed, it's all not usable."

## Actions

1. **Propose the edition without being asked**: The system must assemble a
   candidate edition for the coming week on its own, from the highest-scoring
   approved and pending work, and present it as the default thing to look at.
2. **Send on one approval**: An authorized person must be able to approve the
   proposed edition and have it sent, without assembling, previewing or
   confirming as separate acts.
3. **Say where work went**: Approving must tell the person what happened and
   where the article now is, and offer to go there. The destination exists; the
   signpost does not.
4. **Stop showing one list twice**: The system must not present the same set of
   articles as two different screens.
5. **Say what the machine is doing**: Collection, scoring and assembly must be
   visible as status, with what was collected, when, and what it produced, so
   nobody has to start a run to find out whether one is needed.
6. **Keep the editor's controls**: A person who wants to change the proposal must
   be able to add, remove and reorder its contents, and to reject items, without
   leaving the proposal.
7. **Select in bulk on editions**: The editions list must offer the same
   selection, deselection and delete that the other lists offer.
8. **Never lose work silently**: Any action that removes work from the flow must
   be reversible or confirmed, and must say what happened.

## Preconditions

- Sources are configured and at least one is active and healthy.
- The organization has a template, whether the built-in edition or a stored one.
- The person approving is authorized to send to subscribers.

## Postconditions

- A proposed edition exists for the current week without anyone having asked.
- Approving it sends it, and the record shows who approved and when.
- Approving reports where the article went, and getting there is one step.
- Editions can be selected and deleted in bulk.

## Inputs

- **Collected articles**: What the machine gathered and scored.
- **Approval**: One decision from an authorized person.
- **Editorial changes**: Optional additions, removals and reordering.

## Outputs

- **A proposed edition**: Assembled, previewable, ready to approve.
- **A sent newsletter**: With a record of who approved it.
- **Pipeline status**: What was collected and when, what is waiting, what was
  rejected and why.

## Business Rules

- **BR-009**: When an action moves work off the screen a person is using, the
  interface must say where it went.
- **BR-010**: The product must not require a person to know the order of internal
  steps in order to produce its output.
- **BR-011**: Sending to subscribers requires an explicit human approval, and that
  approval is recorded. Automation may assemble and propose; it may not send.
- **BR-012**: Two screens must not present the same data as though they were
  different things.
- **BR-001**: A setting the product offers must take effect, or must not be
  offered. (Shared with RQ-002 and RQ-003.)

## Dependencies

- Collection running on a schedule rather than on demand.
- Scoring good enough that a proposal is worth reading, which RQ-002 addressed by
  making the model configurable and effective.
- A template that renders a proposal, which RQ-003 made selectable.

## CRUD Operations

- **CREATE**: A proposed edition, once per week, per organization.
- **READ**: The proposal, the pipeline status, and articles at every stage.
- **UPDATE**: The proposal's contents; an article's stage.
- **DELETE**: Editions, individually and in bulk.

## Open questions for the clarify round

1. **Does automation send, or only propose?** **Answered: propose only.** The
   automation assembles and presents; a person approves and that approval is what
   sends. BR-011 stands as written, and no unattended send path is built.
2. **What does the proposal contain when the machine is not confident?**
   **Answered: propose what there is, marked as thin.** The proposal states how
   many were collected and how many rejected, so a light week reads as a light
   week. It never lowers the threshold on its own and never pads itself to look
   full.
3. **Should approving an article still be a separate act at all?**
   **Answered: no.** The edition is the unit of approval. Approving an individual
   article becomes optional editorial refinement, which is what removes the queue
   as a station and leaves it as a view.
4. **What happens to the Feed screen?** **Answered: the Feed becomes the
   proposal.** Opening the product shows this week's edition, already assembled.
   The Review queue stays as a filter of that view for anyone who wants to
   intervene. The Editions screen already carries the pipeline counts, so some of
   this moves rather than being built.
5. **Deleting an edition that was sent: allowed, or blocked?**
   **Answered after evaluating what a delete actually destroys.**

   `EmailEvent.editionId` is a plain String with no relation and no cascade. So
   deleting an edition does not remove its delivery history, it **orphans** it: the
   SENT, OPENED and CLICKED rows survive pointing at an edition that no longer
   exists. That is worse than clean loss, because the counts stay in the data and
   can no longer be attributed to anything. There are 71 delivery events across 19
   editions and zero orphans today, because nobody has deleted a sent edition yet.
   The defect is latent, so it can be closed before it bites.

   The design that follows:

   - **Archive, not delete, for anything sent.** `Edition.archivedAt`, hidden from
     the default list and reachable under a filter. Nothing is destroyed, and this
     is what the bulk bar offers for sent editions.
   - **Delete stays for editions never sent.** A draft has no delivery history, so
     there is nothing to orphan and nothing to preserve.
   - **Force delete of a sent edition: allowed, restricted to OWNER**, and obliged
     to delete that edition's `EmailEvent` rows in the same transaction, since no
     cascade will. The confirmation states the real number: this also destroys N
     delivery records for M recipients.
   - **Fix the latent defect while here**: the existing single-edition DELETE has
     the same hole and should clean up events too.

## Note on scope

This is the largest requirement so far and it should be split once the questions
above are answered. Action 7, bulk selection on the editions list, is independent
of everything else here and can ship on its own; the primitive already exists in
`components/radar/selection.tsx` and is used by four other screens.
